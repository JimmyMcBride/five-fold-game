# HEXES & TRAVEL

`One of the main methods of play in Fivefold is traveling through the overworld, or “wilderness”. The wilderness is a dangerous place, and must be carefully navigated by any characters that don’t want to die before they ever get to their destination.` 

#  **HEXES**

`The world is measured in Hexes. Each hex is 3 miles - or one league - across. A typical, unmodified hex takes two hours to traverse on foot. Not every hex is easy to walk through, though, and there are many conditions that affect how difficult a journey might be through it, as explained later in this section.`

`When traveling, characters can see all major structures in their current hex, and can also see the major structures and conditions affecting the hexes directly bordering the one they are in. This means that travel is made up of a series of informed decisions that eventually will lead them to their destination. Major Structures include things like towns and villages, dungeons, fortresses, temples, etc.` 

`When you rest in the wilderness, you’ll have to address the Cold and Hunger that your characters are subjected to, as described in the previous section.`

## **HEX VARIABLES**

`Hexes can have a multitude of variables that affect the difficulty of travel. These variables can be applied to Hexes randomly, or as the GM wishes.`

**`Positive Variables:`**

* **`Bountiful`**`. Rolls made to find food are made with advantage.`  
* **`Warm`**`. This hex contains a source of heat like a rare flamecloud, volcanic vent, hotspring or a basking skywhale. You do not have to make a Survival roll. Almost all civilized places have this variable.`   
* **`Fuel-Rich`**`. This hex contains a source of fuel, like an old forest, a coal mine, caverns with exposed lava, or a ruined city that hasn’t yet been plundered. Survival rolls are made with advantage, as usable fuel is easier to find.` 

**`Negative Variables:`**

* **`Blizzard`**`. A blizzard is currently blowing through this hex. Traveling through this hex takes twice as long. While in this hex, you cannot see what is in the bordering hexes. Rolls made to find food and Survival rolls are made with disadvantage, and if you lose a recovery die due to failing your Survival roll, you lose an additional die.`  
* **`Freezing`**`. This Hex is particularly cold. Cold Survival rolls must be a Hard success. If you lose a recovery die due to failing your Survival roll, you lose an additional die.`  
* **`Sub-zero`**`. This Hex has been so cold for so long that it will sap the life from the unprepared. Cold Survival rolls must be a Critical Success. If you lose a recovery die due to failing your Survival roll, you lose two additional dice.`  
* **`Desolate`**`. There is almost no wildlife in this hex. Rolls made to find food must be a Hard Success.`

## **TRAVERSAL DIFFICULTY**

`Various biomes make up the world, with each kind of environment potentially having an effect on the travel time. The following is a list of environmental difficulties.`

* **`Easy.`** `Roads, flatlands, limited elevation variations. Traveling through this hex takes 2 hours.`  
* **`Moderate.`** `Hills, iced-over rivers and lakes, ruined cities,  naval graveyards, tunnels. Traveling through this kind of hex takes 4 hours.`  
* **`Hard.`** `Mountains, Volcanic crags, deep caverns, massive and labyrinthian ruins. These hexes take 8 hours to travel through.`

# **TRAVEL SPEED**

`A group can only travel as fast as their slowest member. The traversal difficulties describe how long it takes to travel through each kind of Hex on foot. While riding a mount, the travel time is cut in half.`

`Characters can travel for eight hours before they need to rest. They can travel for longer, but every hour they do so, they must Hard succeed on a Heart Roll or lose a Recovery Die.`

# **MAPPING AN ADVENTURE**

`Creating a map for the players to adventure in is fairly simple. It is recommended to start with a local area, about 32 x 32 hexes. Mark each hex with a difficulty, which should be informed by what is in it. Then consider the variables that might be applied to those hexes. Which ones are constant? Is the blizzard to the north always present? Is there a 6-league-wide volcanic biome that is always warm to the east? Is it isolated from the rest of the map by a mountain range that can be more easily bypassed by a tunnel carved by the Hares that live there? Make sure to plot out a few civilized areas - a village, a town, and a monastery or two - and put some dungeons on the map (See the Dungeons section in this book for advice on how to craft those). To seed the map with adventure, make sure to keep some of it hidden from your players until they discover it. Give them rumors about a dungeon that is somewhere to the south of their village that supposedly has a stockpile of Scrolls, or a quest to find a circle to the old gods near the northern monastery that has a powerful sword, still stuck in the altar.` 

`Ultimately, it is up to you, but a good map will reward exploration. And exploration is best experienced when discovery is emphasized, and obtained through informed decisions. The players will have fun if they know the reason their characters are wealthier and stronger is because of the series of decisions they made to get them there.`
