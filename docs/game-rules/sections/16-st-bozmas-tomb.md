# ST BOZMA’S TOMB

## **An Adventure for 3-5 Level 1 Characters**

`In this adventure, the player characters begin in the small village of Blackbrook during an annual celebration of the Sheltering Feast. A nearby shrine, tended to by the monks of Saint Bozma, are attacked by a mysterious tribe of the Scorched that have brought demons in tow. A monk runs back to Blackbrook to tell the village what happened, and the town’s Elder asks the party to help deal with the threat. The players will discover that the tribe attacked the shrine because the real body of Saint Bozma is buried in a hidden tomb beneath it, uncorrupted by rot and preserved in her glass coffin. The Scorched want to take the body and use the Saint’s bones in a ritual to bind a powerful demon within their flamecloud. While the players are in this tomb, an occultist leads a group of demons and scorched into Blackbrook to raid it. The players come back to town to see the destruction, and now have a villain to bring to justice, if they wish to continue the campaign.`

#### **`WHAT LEAD TO THIS?`**

`Clan Syphax are a Scorched Tribe from the tundra that recently lost their flamecloud. The flamecallers of their clan had no answer as to why or how to get the cloud to ignite once again, and they were on a ticking clock. It didn’t take long for the clan to start freezing to death, and in desperation, their Khan, a Caprica named Manessa, consulted an occultist for help. The occultist, Barnabe Fearstricken, reached beyond the veil and a demon named Valefor answered, and instructed Manessa to bring him the bones of Saint Bozma. The demon promised that he could reignite the flames of the cloud by using the power in those bones, and so Manessa brought his warriors to the Shrine of Saint Bozma to take what he needed. His concern is firstly for his tribe, and will do everything in his power to save them. Unfortunately, the monks are unwilling to hand over the key to enter Saint Bozma’s entombment chamber, and so he slaughters them.`

`When the adventure begins, Manessa is already at the Shrine with his band of raiders, puzzling over how to enter the vault. His warriors have already angered the protective angels that guard the Saint’s body, and by the time the players get there, will have quite the challenge to overcome.` 

`In the village of Blackbrook, though, the commoners have gathered what little they have to contribute to the Sheltering Feast. This holiday is an eight-day-long celebration of the birth of Jirah. People all around the world celebrate by decorating with evergreen wreaths and paper ornaments that sport hand-drawn symbols of prayer. Double-wound candles are kept lit through the entire week in every church, and inns offer free meals and rooms to travelers in the spirit of the holiday. Blackbrook is pulling together the final touches to start the holiday with a meal.` 

#### **`THE PACE OF THE ADVENTURE`**

1. `Introduce the players to the village of Blackbrook. It is likely they are all at the inn or gathering hall, enjoying a meal together or perhaps helping the villagers gather the feast together. Describe the village to the players and ask them what they are all doing in town. Figure out the reason they came, the reason they might help with a coming adventure, and how they all know each other (if they do).`   
2. `When the pace just starts to reach a lull, Run A Friar Crier. The characters can plan for their travel, prepare, and set out.`  
3. `Run travel through the overland. The Shrine of Saint Bozma is 4 leagues away.`   
4. `Run the Dungeon Saint Bozma’s Tomb.`  
5. `After the dungeon, the players may wish to return to town. When they do, run Blackbrook in Smoke.`

#### **`BLACKBROOK`**

`Blackbrook is a quaint little Lupii village of approximately 200 people, nestled on the outskirts of an evergreen forest. Blackbrook is named for the long-frozen river that runs beneath the town’s gathering hall, which was once an arched stone bridge across this motionless river. Beneath the thick ice is the town’s claim to fame: a shining shield, frozen in suspension over the smooth rocks of the stilled riverbed. This shield is called Blackbrook’s Aegis, and the church treats it as a sacred relic that protects the town from the worst demons, so long as they keep the lanterns on the bridge above it lit. And indeed, the town has taken that tradition very seriously; the lanterns are locked behind intricate cages that only the local priest, Brother Alban, has the keys to. He refills the oil of the lanterns nightly. The only time in living memory that they went out was three years ago, during the Sheltering Feast, and demons attacked the town on that very night.`   
`Blackbrook sustains itself with its herd of milkmice, and has made a deal with the nearby tribes of Sabertooth elves that live in the forest; the citizens of Blackbrook trade milkmouse meat, leather, and cheese in exchange for lumber and skywhale products like fat and meat. Blackbrook has a Church, an inn called The Hanging Holly, the Gathering Hall, a General Store, and about 3 dozen houses that belong to the citizens. Most citizens are milkmouse farmers, and so the herds of mice can be seen in the flat fields around the town. The town’s buildings are mostly made from hardened chitin and insulated with milkmouse furs on the inside, as is standard of House Lupii.` 

# **CHARACTERS**

1. **`Elder Gratiana.`** `An elderly Sinister Elf woman that leads Blackbrook as an advisor. She is hunched over and grandmotherly, and highly hospitable. She was a child when the sun set three hundred years ago.`  
2. **`Brother Alban.`** `Brother Alban was once the apprentice of Paladin Zeru, but when Zeru died in a demon attack on Blackbrook three years ago, Alban took up the position as priest at the church. He is a human in his early thirties, and tries not to let show that he is haunted by what happened to his master.`

3. **`Cinnamon-Stick.`** `Cinnamon-Stick is a Sabertooth Elf innkeeper. He used to be a whaler, and he displays his harpy’s saddle and his old harpoon gun above the hearth of The Hanging Holly. He is very friendly, and loves sharing stories of the past, and hearing stories of travelers that pass through on the way to Sinestra.`   
4. **`Secunda & Sil.`** `Secunda is the widow of Zeru and the mother of Sil. They are both Corvids, and work as farmhands for the herd of milkmice. Sil just turned 10, and she is a rather excitable little girl that goes through bouts of sadness around this time of year, since that is when her father died.` 

## **EVENT: A FRIAR CRIER**

`The Sheltering Feast in Blackbrook smells of pine ale and heart stew. The aroma of bone broth warms the candlelit gathering hall as villagers bring their contributions to the feast. Hugs are given to family and neighbors and gifts are exchanged, with foreigners even getting home-made trinkets to commemorate their time spent in blackbrook. Sil has made pine and holly crowns for the pilgrims and visitors, and hands them out with joy. The warmth of the holiday is in full swing when the doors to the gathering hall suddenly swing open. Brother Linus, a monk from the Shrine of Saint Bozma, has just arrived on the back of his strider beetle with nasty burn wounds on his arms and an arrow in his back. He limps into the gathering hall and collapses, asking for help: a tribe of Scorched brought demons to the shrine and killed the other monks there. He is branded with a mark on his back, which was put there by Barnabe before Linus escaped. It is the sigil of Valefor, the demon duke of thieves (identified with a hard Mind roll concerning the occult). The shrine is near the village, and Elder Gratiana will ask the players to investigate. Both Brother Linus and Gratiana promise a reward to the players if they can save the shrine.`

#### **`SAINT BOZMA’S TOMB`**

`Unbeknownst to the monks that lived at this monastery, Saint Bozma’s body is housed in a locked, indestructible glass casket beneath the shrine, and it is being protected by a holy oath that can only be released if someone prays a specific prayer. Her body is perfectly preserved, untouched by decay. Below are the details of each major location in this area, starting with what the players will likely see first.`

## **THE MONASTERY GROUNDS**

`Positioned atop a snowy hill sits the picturesque Monastery of Saint Bozma. This monastery normally houses about two dozen monks that live on the grounds. The Shrine itself is a long stone building with three smoking chimneys and beautiful stained glass windows. The windows depict Saint Bozma, a Hare with a shining halo and regal Argeshian robes. Surrounding the shrine are four cedar Cabins with chitin roofs and a small storage shack. When the players approach, they will see about ten dead monks that darken the snow with their blood, and two Scorched Raiders looting the Storage Shack. Beyond the Shrine and just a little higher on the hill lies the Apiary. It is an old watchtower that is covered in a papery hive and surrounded by smoking pillars.` 

`If the players succeed on a roll made to search or scout the grounds, or tell you that they are searching in a way you find convincing, they will find a Cave that they can enter at the bottom of the hill, hidden almost entirely by a collapsed snowbank.`

## **CABINS**

`The cabins are all very simple. If the players search the cabins, they can find 1d10 rations, 4d10 gold, 1d10 fuel, and The Incorruptible Prayer Book.`

***`Incorruptible Prayer Book.`** `This Prayer Book contains Argeshian prayers that have been passed down from the gospel of Saint Bozma. They are prayers about purity and keeping one’s self sanctified. This Prayer Book has one slot that is permanently filled with a Tier 2 Scroll of Healing. When you use the Liturgy ability with this book, you can remove one condition affecting your targeted ally.`*

## **STORAGE SHACK**

`This small cedarwood building has been almost entirely emptied by the Syphax clan. There are barrels of frozen hell hornet jelly, various furs and leathers, and if a player rolls a hard success (Mind) to search the building, they find 5 rations and a Bozman Sensor.` 

***`Bozman Sensor.`** `The sensor acts as a lightsource that can burn for 2 hours/fuel spent, and emits a pillar of dark red smoke when lit. Holding a lit sensor will prevent Hellhornets from becoming hostile against you.`* **

`There are two Scorched Raiders going through the shack, as described previously, and will attack on sight unless one of the players is a Caprica, speaks Caprica to them, or does something else to prevent immediate hostility.` 

## **APIARY**

`Once, this structure was a watchtower built by the Sinistre Empire as part of a fortress that used to be here. Now, it is entirely covered in a papery tumor of a hive that a colony of Hellhornets call home. These giant, cobalt wasps are predatory in nature and hunt small animals. Their hive is covered with a swarm of their warriors that will beat their wings against the surface in unison as a warning to anyone that gets too close. There is a circle of smoking lanterns around the apiary, and if a character crosses that threshold without a Bozman Sensor, the hornets will attack. It is possible, with some creative thinking - and maybe a few rolls - for the players to use the hornets to their advantage somehow. As with most things when GMing, be flexible with the players.`

## **SHRINE**

`The shrine itself is a long stone building with three smoking chimneys and beautiful stained glass windows. The windows depict Saint Bozma, a Hare with a shining halo and regal Argeshian robes. It has chitin doors that wear curtains of white milkmouse fur as insulation. Entering the building leads to the Sanctuary.`

1. **`Sanctuary.`** `This long room smells strongly of incense, alcohol, and a distinct sweetness not unlike honey. It is dotted with a dozen wooden columns, each displaying a colorfully painted sculpture of one of the other Saints that followed Jirah. The columns section off two narrow halls from the main, pew-filled middle of the shrine. They all face the middle of the room, which holds the Altar of Saint Bozma. On the Altar, written in Argeshian, is the phrase “May the pure of heart gain their righteousness; and may the impure come to be sanctified.” A character can pray at the altar once per day. If they have any Sin, praying at the altar removes all of their sin. If they have no Sin, the character receives Saint Bozma’s Blessing. Until the start of the next day, they are immune to Poison damage and any effects that poison features cause.` 

   `Beyond the Sanctuary is a burning hearth with a wall of furred curtains on either side that hide the Brewery.`  
2. **`Brewery.`** `This room is a split level, with a balcony that wraps around 4 large kegs of Hellhornet Mead. These massive barrels are used by the monks to ferment the jelly of their Hellhornets from the apiary. There is nothing particularly special about this mead, other than its sweet and sour taste. The monks produced this mead as a way to stave off their hunger when fasting, and as a revenue source by selling their excess to Blackbrook and travelers. The balcony has smaller barrels of Jelly and water, and there is a Bozman Sensor on a shelf amongst the supplies. The balcony overlooks a basement level, where the monks bottle their mead. There are several more Scorched Raiders accompanied by a Zeboul on the ground floor, drinking up the bottles of mead. The Scorched Raiders are drunk, so players have advantage on defensive rolls against their weapon attacks, and advantage on their own weapon attacks against the raiders. There is a set of stairs that leads down into the Cellar.`  
3. **`Cellar.`** `The cellar is a modest library with a fireplace, milkmouse fur rugs, and a few tables and chairs for reading. Four bookshelves line the northern wall, with one of them pulled away from it and knocked down. The books contain copies of letters sent to and from different shrines, recipes for mead and other foods, and commentaries on holy texts. The wall that the knocked-over shelf once covered has been broken through by the Syphax Clan, and is now a passageway into the once hidden Tomb entry. There are two dead monks in this room, clutching one another in an embrace with a single spear shoved through them both. The larger of the two was trying to protect the smaller one, and the smaller one is still clutching a Healing Potion.`   
4. **`Tomb Entry.`** `This room is far older than most of the building, and was walled off centuries ago. A layer of dust covers the ground, and there is a frozen well on the left side of the room. This well acts as a passage into the Caves below, and players that took the path through the caverns enter into the tomb from here. On the right side of the room, there are four narrow halls, with three rows of three coffins each. These are the coffins of the handmaidens of Bozma. Opening the coffins or disturbing them will summon a Throne. The coffins hold prayer books, two random tier 1 scrolls, and 5d10 gold. If the gold is taken selfishly, the stealing player gains 1 sin, or 2 if they are Greed viced. The Throne will warn against stealing the gold, if it sees the character make an attempt, and will become hostile if the character steals it anyway.` 

## **ANGELIC TEST**

`This tomb is sacred, and is protected by angels that took an oath to protect Saint Bozma’s tomb when she was first interred. The first time the players come into contact with an angel in the tomb, it will attempt to measure the moral worth of the characters. The party must pass a hard group Soul roll, with each character’s roll increased by an amount equal to their sin, or else all the angels in the tomb will view the party as intruders on the tomb and will be hostile. If the party succeeds, the angels will only be hostile toward the Syphax clan and their demons. If there is a priest in the party, the angel will address them directly and ask for an explanation. As long as the priest (or party) doesn’t say anything absolutely damning, the Angel is likely to take the priest at their word.` 

---

`There is a secret door that can be triggered at the end of one of the narrow halls. It can be found with a hard roll to search the area, and opens up into a long, turning passageway that leads to both the locked room in the Groundskeeper’s Storage and further down to a secret door in Bozma’s Resting Chamber. There is a door to the north that enters into the Gallery, one that leads to the Groundskeeper’s Storage to the east, and one to the west that leads to the Paladin’s Prayer Room.` 

5. **`Gallery.`** `This room has various scenes carved into the row of stones in the middle of the wall’s height, and intricately woven tapestries hanging above those scenes. Each art piece depicts a different miracle, either from Jirah’s time, or done by the hands of Saint Bozma. There is a bloodtrail leading from the middle of this room to the door toward the west, which leads into the Paladin’s Prayer Room. A door to the east leads toward the Groundskeeper’s Storage, and the northern door is locked, but leads to Saint Bozma’s Resting Chamber. This door has a slot that could hold the handle of a Bozman Sensor perfectly, and beneath the slot, there is a scripture written: “The praises of his people rise up to him like a sweet burning incense.” It can be unlocked by either a hard group heart roll, a critical Heart roll of someone with a heart score over 60, or by placing a lit Bozman Sensor in the slot.`   
6. **`Paladin’s Prayer Room.`** `This room has a prayer stool in its middle that is surrounded by a coil of threads that were once being woven into an elaborate tapestry. The tapestry is unfinished, but depicts half of Saint Bozma’s body. On the prayer stool are two Gilded Kneeplates. These kneeplates still shine after all this time, and can be attached to light or heavy leg armor to give that piece of armor one more slot.`  
   	`Also in this room, the bloodtrail ends. It ends on a particularly large Zeboul that is feasting upon the remains of four different monks. Blood and gore soaks the room from his wicked feast. He will attack on sight, unless there is an occultist in the party, in which case he will attempt to barter for his life. The Zeboul has 75 hit points and rolls 2d10 momentum each turn, instead of 1d10. He has a Bozman Sensor and 5d10 gold.`  
7. **`Groundskeeper’s Storage.`** `This room contains an old worn bed, a wood stove, and the frozen remains of furniture. Behind a locked door of iron bars is a weapon (GM’s pick) with a Tier 1 Holy Scroll slotted into it. This door is locked, but the lock can be picked with a normal success. This room can also be entered from the secret passageway described in the Tomb entry section.`  
8. **`Saint Bozma’s Resting Chamber.`** `This is the final room of the tomb, and contains the raised glass casket of Saint Bozma. She is preserved perfectly, and looks as if she is not even dead, but only asleep. She is an albino hare, and wears black and white robes that have scriptures in Argeshian written upon them. Beneath her casket is a stone tablet with slotted cubbies that hold 3-5 random Tier 1 scrolls.`   
   	`Inside this room, Barnabe, Manessa, two Scorched Raiders and a Scorched Flamecaller work to open the casket. Barnabe is attempting to decipher the correct prayer to utter that will release the holy ward that protects the saint’s body. Manessa cares primarily about the life of his tribe, and while what he is doing is wrong, he can be reasoned with if the players find a way to convince him to find another way. Barnabe, however, is a weasel of a man, and serves himself first, and Valefor second, and he knows that Valefor will reward him if he is able to get Bozma’s bones. If combat begins and the players are fighting both Manessa’s clan and Barnabe, Barnabe will stay in the far rank as much as he can, and will use his full turn to try to decode the prayer. If he is put into the near rank, he will use his action to switch ranks again and use his ability. On each of his turns that he is able to stay on the far rank, he gains rolls 4 momentum die (2 in the near rank). Every other GM maneuver should be Barnabe’s Decode. Using Decode 3 times will open the casket, and he will attempt to flee with the corpse. Barnabe has 75 health, but has a cursed amulet that binds him to Valefor and gives him 25 temporary health at the start of his turns (non-stacking). He is a coward and will not raise a finger to deal damage himself`  
   	`If combat begins and Manessa is convinced to no longer use Barnabe’s help, Barnabe will summon 3 Zeboul and fight on his own. If the players are defeated or flee, he will take the Saint’s body as before.`  
   	`Either way, after 4 maneuvers (player or GM), a Throne is summoned into combat. If the players successfully placated the angels already, it will automatically deal its damage from its weapon attack to one enemy target, and draw their attention. They will all attack the angel first, unless they are taunted, and the angel will disappear after being damaged 3 times. The player with the highest soul stat will make soul rolls for the angel to see if each enemy attack hits or misses. If the players have not placated the Angels, then use a normal Throne stat block. Flip a coin - on heads, it spends its turn automatically dealing damage to one of the enemies, and on tails, it takes a normal turn against the players.`   
   	`If the players win, Manessa drops his weapon: Spear of the Desperate Khan. This spear is permanently slotted with a tier 1 Flame Scroll, and deals an additional 1d10 Flame damage if the wielder is below half their maximum health.`   
   `Barnabe drops 500 gold pieces and an amulet with the Sigil of Valefor upon it, which is identifiable with a Hard Mind roll to identify occultic/demonic things. If worn, the wearer hears the maddening voice of Valefor, and must make a critical soul roll to take it off, repeatable once per day. It also grants them the ability Valefor’s Profane Shield, which grants the wearer 25 temporary hit points.`
