# TRIUMPHS & FAME

`Triumphs are a system of narrative rewards that players get on their adventures and spend during their downtime. Fame is a measure of how recognizable the player characters are throughout the realm. This is perhaps the most flexible system in this entire game, as players may choose to spend their triumphs on things that are listed nowhere in this book. The examples listed in this section are just that: examples. Suggestions to get the brain going for both players and GMs, but if a player has an idea that could be reasonably tied into this system, then the GM should find a way to let that happen.`

## **TRIUMPHS**

`Triumphs are gained in the following ways:`

* `The characters succeed in a combat encounter where the combined sum of the enemy levels is equal to or greater than the average character level of the party.`   
* `A quest or adventure is completed.`   
* `The characters find notable treasure.`   
* `A character accomplishes a goal related to their calling or significantly makes progress toward it.`

`This list is non-exhaustive, and the GM might want to give out Triumphs for things that aren’t listed here. As a rule of thumb, a Triumph should be rewarded when it feels like the players have accomplished something worth bragging about. If the characters can go back home, have a drink at the local bar and tell stories about how they’ve overcome the obstacle - that is worth a Triumph! And, a little GM advice? Prompt your players to do exactly that when they finally get to a safe haven and rest.`  
	`Spending Triumphs can only be done in a safe place while resting up. Anywhere that counts as a safe enough place to rest and regain their lost Recovery Dice is an appropriate place, typically. Triumphs are spent over the course of that rest period, and are used for things that will tie the character more deeply into the setting. The GM should ask the players to narrate how they go about these tasks. Note that there is no chance of failure for these - if a character spends a Triumph to build a house in their favorite village? They succeed. There may be complications, but it is up to the player to narrate how their character overcomes those issues, and by the end of the rest period, a house is built! The players should all talk amongst themselves and decide on how long they want to spend in this rest period spending their Triumphs. It can be a few days, a few weeks, or even years if all the players agree. The time it takes for each task the players want to achieve should be respected, though, as a home is not likely to be built in a single day, nor is a campaign to lower taxes in the kingdom, nor a plot to plant seeds that brew the first stages of a political coup done in just a week. These downtime activities are likely to take a good long while in-universe, so pan out and hit the high notes in your narration of the events, and save the knitty-gritty details for the scenes you really think deserve to be played out.`  
	`Another rule of thumb on how to balance the Triumphs system is to scale the amount spent with the result. Spending a single Triumph is something that temporarily affects a small group of people. Think throwing a feast for a village where everyone gets to have a full belly for one night. You will earn the good rapport of the locals, and certainly become more well known to them, but the effects are more temporary and less material.`   
`Two Triumphs might be something more like building a small house for you and your family, or repairing the damages sustained in a battle to a town or village, or bringing in a small herd of milkmice to an area to help or even start a local farm - something that really starts to have a lasting impact on a small community.`   
`For three Triumphs, you start to impact an entire hex or advance your character in more significant ways. You may clear and lumber an entire league of trees from the pines, train a standing militia, seek out a squire or apprentice to disciple, or begin researching new techniques, spells, or obscure knowledge, or pursue being knighted.`  
`Four Triumphs and you are looking at affecting a small region. Your sphere of influence can really start to grow at this point. You might build a castle, petition a local king for rights to collect taxes, become the commander of an army or get a favor from a major faction that you can cash in later.`   
`Many of the things the characters gain through spending their Triumphs are likely to be rich seeds for more adventures or great roleplay moments. GMs and players should work with each other on these moments to make sure that when Triumphs are spent, the outcome the player wants is clearly understood. There may be circumstances where it would break the immersion of the world to spend triumphs in a specific way - but that means there is an adventure just waiting to be had so that the player can remove the obstacles in the way of their goals, and can spend their hard-earned Triumphs once their chance of success has been cemented. For example, you may want to build a castle on a mountaintop, but the GM knows that your ideal mountaintop is actually the secret home of a dragon. You obviously must take care of that problem before your workers can lay the foundations and pile up stone, and so you suddenly have an adventure on your hands! GMs shouldn’t just make up obstacles on the fly to prevent the use of Triumphs, though. The players already earned these points by going through other adventures, and this is their chance to take the wheel on the narrative and steer the world toward a place that is shaped by the actions of their characters. Let them. They will love the setting more for it.`

## **FAME**

`Fame goes hand-in-hand with Triumphs. As stated above, it is an abstracted measure of how well known your character is throughout the realm. It typically will start at 1, but certain backgrounds (like noble birth, for example) could have a character starting with higher fame. You gain a number of fame every time you level up, equal to your new level. So when you hit level 2, you gain 2 fame. When you reach level 3, you gain 3, and so on. You also gain 1 fame for every Triumph you spend during your downtime.`   
	`Fame is the limiter on how many Triumphs your character can spend at once. Being an abstraction of your character’s reputation, it is often directly proportional to the kinds of opportunities the character will have access to. A character can always spend all their Triumphs during a rest period, but they can only spend a number of them on a single task equal to their Fame/10, rounded down. So at 10 Fame and below the character can only spend one Triumph on a task, and therefore, reap small benefits. At 20 Fame, they can spend 2 on a task, since their reputation has grown and people are more willing to work with them. At 30, 3, and so on. This will prevent a low-level nobody from beating up some bad guys, turning in a few small quests and suddenly building a castle and becoming the local lord.`   
	`Fame can also be used as a stat to roll against. If the GM thinks that there is a chance someone might have heard of your character’s exploits but isn’t sure, then they might call for a Fame roll. Just like other stats, rolling under your Fame means the character has heard of you and your deeds, while rolling above it likely means they haven’t. Other circumstances might take your Fame into account, too, though that will be more obvious in play, as I can’t list every single circumstance here. As in all things, be flexible.` 

## **USING TRIUMPHS FOR NEW PERKS?**

	`Some players might want to use their Triumphs to create new techniques that their character learns or invents. A Magus might want to research a new spell, or an occultist might want to spend time pouring through the dusty tomes of the old libraries in Sinestra trying to decode the name of a demon to summon them. I think this is perfectly fine, but these things should not be easy. Here is my suggestions on how to let players do this without it breaking the balance of the game:`

* `Respect the existing perks. Look at the perks that are already in the game - if there is already a perk that does what they are trying to do, then tell the player to take that perk on their next level up.`  
* `Define what their new perk is going to do. Compare it to the existing perks and make a call on what the stat prerequisite would be.`   
* `Figure out how many triumphs the player needs to spend for it. A perk that is about as strong as a prerequisite 35 should probably take at least 6 Triumphs, with something on par with a prerequisite 85 taking no less than 16. I’d allow the players to spend Triumphs on these over multiple rest periods, and just have them keep track of how close they are to unlocking their new perk.`
