# Fivefold

# **BASIC RULES**

---

## **STATS & MODIFIERS**

`Each character in Fivefold has 5 primary stats that they use to determine their likelihood of success on a task. These are Soul, Heart, Mind, Reflex, and Voice. These stats reflect the different physical and mental attributes of a character, and are measured in numbers between 0 and 85. Intuitively, the higher the stat, the better a character is with any skills or tasks related to that stat.`

**`Heart`**`: Your bodily strength and resilience. A character’s Heart stat contributes to their total health. When your heart stat increases, your maximum health increases by the same amount.`  
**`Mind`**`: Your worldly intellect and intuition. Investigating clues, lore, and the occult.`  
**`Reflex`**`: Your dexterity and speed.`  
**`Soul`**`: Your moral compass, and resilience of spirit. Your ability to stay in touch with your own humanity, and divinity.`  
**`Voice`**`: Your social aptitude. How good you are at convincing people to see your point of view, or lying.` 

## **MODIFIERS**

`Each stat has a Modifier that is used in various ways, both in and out of combat. The Modifier is equal to 10% of the stat, rounded down. For example, if your Mind Stat is 78, your Mind Modifier is 7.`

## **NATURAL STATS**

`Stats cannot normally go above 90 for any mortal character, but certain conditional effects, perks, and abilities can raise them above 90 temporarily. Sometimes, the rules will specify "Natural" stats. A Natural stat is the value without any of these increases or decreases from effects, gear, abilities, etc.` 

## **ROLLING**

`Your GM will tell you when you should attempt a roll and how difficult the task is.`

* `A normal task requires a roll of equal to or less than your stat on 1D100 (a Normal success).`  
* `A difficult task requires a roll result equal to or less than half your stat (a Hard success).`  
* `A task approaching the limits of mortal capability requires a roll equal to or less than the Modifier for the rolled stat (a Critical success).`

## **CRITICAL FAILURE**

`If a roll is ever naturally 96 or higher, it is always a failure. Sometimes the GM may wish to make this kind of failure particularly dramatic - you drop your weapon, your pants split, you spill your drink in a king’s lap, etc.`

## **RECOVERY DIE**

`Each character has a number of Recovery Dice equal to their level + (their highest stat modifier x 2). These dice are used to heal wounds and recover. When a character spends a Recovery Die, they roll a d10 + their Heart Modifier, and regain health equal to the result. Recovery Dice can be spent in the following ways:`

**`Patch Up.`** `After completing a combat encounter, you can roll a single Recovery Die.`  
**`Rest.`** `When you end your day and rest, you can spend as many Recovery Dice as you would like.` 

`Characters only regain their Recovery Dice when they rest in a village, town, city, or some other safe and well-stocked environment. They regain a number of Recovery Dice equal to their level per day spent resting in a town.`

## **CHARACTER DEATH**

`When a player character reaches 0 hit points, they begin Dying. A Dying character can do everything a normal character can do, but because they are bleeding out, it comes at a cost. If Dying a character uses an Ability, Maneuver, or Action, they lose a Recovery Die. They also lose one each time they take damage. The moment the character's health is above 0, they are no longer Dying.` 

`If a Dying character has no more Recovery Die left, they are dead.`

## **LIGHT**

`Typically, carrying a light source is important. When exploring, rolls made that rely on sight are made with disadvantage if you do not have a nearby lightsource. When fighting, weapon attacks have disadvantage if there is not a nearby light source. Only one character needs to hold a lightsource during combat, but if that character dies or drops the light for any reason, the battlefield darkens.`
